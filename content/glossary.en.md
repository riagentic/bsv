# Dictionary

Words kept in their original Hebrew, Aramaic or Greek form in this translation —
what they mean and what older Bibles (KJV) wrote instead.

## Names of God

### Yahweh

Hebrew יהוה (YHWH). The personal name of the God of Israel, revealed to Moses
(Exod 3:14–15); related to _hayah_, "to be" — "he who is / he who causes to be".
KJV: "the LORD" (small capitals), "JEHOVAH". Jews traditionally do not pronounce
it and say _Adonai_ ("my Lord").

### Yahweh of Hosts

Hebrew יהוה צְבָאוֹת _Yahweh Tzeva'ot_. Yahweh as commander of the armies of heaven
(angels, stars) and of Israel. KJV: "the LORD of hosts".

### Lord Yahweh

Hebrew אֲדֹנָי יהוה _Adonai Yahweh_ — "my Lord Yahweh". KJV: "Lord GOD".

### Yah

Hebrew יָהּ. Short, poetic form of Yahweh (Exod 15:2; Psalms). Heard in
_Hallelu-yah_ and in names like Eliyahu (Elijah). KJV: "JAH", "the LORD".

### Elohim

Hebrew אֱלֹהִים. The ordinary word for "God". Plural in form ("gods", "divine
beings"), but used with singular verbs for the one God of Israel — a plural of
majesty and fullness. Also used for other gods, divine beings (Ps 82:1) and
judges. KJV: "God".

### Eloah

Hebrew אֱלוֹהַּ. The singular of _Elohim_, "God"; poetic and rare — mostly in Job
(41×). KJV: "God".

### Shaddai

Hebrew שַׁדַּי, often _El Shaddai_. Ancient title of God, traditionally "the
Almighty"; possibly linked to "mountain" or "breast/nourisher". KJV: "the
Almighty".

### Yahweh-yireh

Hebrew "Yahweh will see / will provide" — Abraham's name for the mountain of the
binding of Isaac (Gen 22:14). KJV: "Jehovah-jireh".

### Yahweh-nissi

Hebrew "Yahweh is my banner" — Moses' altar after the victory over Amalek (Exod
17:15). KJV: "Jehovah-nissi".

### Yahweh-shalom

Hebrew "Yahweh is peace" — Gideon's altar (Judg 6:24). KJV: "Jehovah-shalom".

### Immanu-El

Hebrew עִמָּנוּ אֵל — "God (is) with us" (Isa 7:14; Matt 1:23). KJV: "Immanuel /
Emmanuel".

## Yeshua and the New Testament

### Yeshua

Hebrew/Aramaic יֵשׁוּעַ, short form of Yehoshua (Joshua) — "Yahweh saves". The name
Jesus was actually called; Greek _Iēsous_, Latin _Iesus_, English "Jesus". See
Matt 1:21: "he will save his people".

### Mashiach

Hebrew מָשִׁיחַ — "anointed one": the king (or priest) anointed with oil, and the
promised king of David's line. Greek translation _Christos_, from which
"Christ". It is a title, not a surname. KJV: "Christ", "Messiah".

### Ruach HaQodesh

Hebrew רוּחַ הַקֹּדֶשׁ — "the Spirit of holiness", the Holy Spirit. _Ruach_ means
spirit, wind and breath at once. KJV: "the Holy Ghost / Holy Spirit".

### Kefa

Aramaic כֵּיפָא _Kefa_ — "rock". The name Yeshua gave to Simon (John 1:42); Greek
_Petros_, English "Peter". KJV: "Peter", "Cephas".

### Miryam

Hebrew מִרְיָם — Miriam, the Hebrew form of "Mary".

### Sha'ul

Hebrew שָׁאוּל — "asked for". The Hebrew name of the apostle Paul (Greek/Latin
_Paulos/Paulus_), and of Israel's first king, Saul.

### Bar-Yeshua

Aramaic "son of Yeshua" — the magician on Cyprus (Acts 13:6). KJV: "Bar-jesus".

### Abba

Aramaic אַבָּא — "father", the warm family word a child uses (Mark 14:36; Rom
8:15).

### Rabbi · Rabbouni

Hebrew/Aramaic "my great one", "my teacher"; _Rabbouni_ is the more intimate,
emphatic form (John 20:16). KJV: "Master", "Rabboni".

### Hosanna

Hebrew הוֹשִׁיעָה נָּא _hoshi'a-na_ — "save, please!" (Ps 118:25); by Yeshua's time a
shout of praise and welcome.

### Amen

Hebrew אָמֵן — "firm, sure, so be it". Said to confirm a prayer or oath; Yeshua
uses it to open solemn sayings ("Amen, I say to you").

### Hallelujah

Hebrew הַלְלוּ־יָהּ — "praise Yah!" (Psalms, Rev 19). KJV: "Praise ye the LORD",
"Alleluia".

### Satan

Hebrew שָׂטָן — "adversary, accuser". In the Old Testament often a title ("the
accuser", Job 1; Zech 3); in the New Testament the name of the devil.

### Beelzebul

Greek form of _Ba'al Zevul_, "lord of the lofty house / prince Baal" — a name
for the ruler of demons (Matt 10:25). KJV: "Beelzebub" (from 2 Kgs 1:2 _Ba'al
Zevuv_, "lord of flies").

### Sanhedrin

Greek _synedrion_, "sitting together" — the supreme Jewish council in Jerusalem
(71 members, led by the high priest). KJV: "the council".

### Golgotha

Aramaic גֻּלְגַּלְתָּא — "skull"; the place of the crucifixion outside Jerusalem. Latin:
_Calvaria_, "Calvary".

### Akeldama

Aramaic "field of blood" — the field bought with Judas' silver (Acts 1:19). KJV:
"Aceldama".

### Immerser · immerse

Greek _baptizō_ = "to dip, immerse, plunge". "Yochanan the Immerser" = John the
Baptist. KJV: "baptize", "Baptist".

### Talitha koum

Aramaic "little girl, get up!" (Mark 5:41).

### Ephphatha

Aramaic "be opened!" (Mark 7:34).

### Eloi, Eloi, lema sabachthani

Aramaic "My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?" — Yeshua quoting Ps 22:1 on
the cross (Mark 15:34; Matthew has Hebrew _Eli, Eli_).

## The dead and the unseen world

### Sheol

Hebrew שְׁאוֹל — the underworld, the grave, where all the dead go, righteous and
wicked alike; a place of silence, not of torment. KJV: "the grave", "hell", "the
pit".

### Hades

Greek ᾅδης — the Greek equivalent of _Sheol_: the realm of the dead. KJV:
"hell".

### Gehenna

Greek γέεννα from Hebrew _Gei Hinnom_, "Valley of Hinnom" — a valley south of
Jerusalem, defiled by child sacrifice to Molech (Jer 7:31) and later a symbol of
God's final judgment. KJV: "hell", "hell fire".

### Tartarus

Greek τάρταρος — in Greek thought the deepest prison below Hades; used once, for
the confinement of fallen angels (2 Pet 2:4). KJV: "hell".

### Abaddon · Apollyon

Hebrew אֲבַדּוֹן "destruction, place of ruin" (Job, Proverbs); in Rev 9:11 the angel
of the abyss, in Greek _Apollyon_, "destroyer".

### Nephilim

Hebrew נְפִילִים — mysterious mighty men of old (Gen 6:4; Num 13:33), perhaps
"fallen ones". KJV: "giants".

### Rephaim

Hebrew רְפָאִים — (1) an ancient people of tall warriors in Canaan; (2) in poetry,
the "shades" — the dead in Sheol (Isa 14:9). KJV: "giants", "the dead".

### Anakim · Emim · Zamzummim

Names of ancient tall peoples of Canaan and Transjordan (Deut 2). KJV:
"Anakims", "Emims", "Zamzummims".

### Leviathan

Hebrew לִוְיָתָן — the great sea monster / twisting sea serpent, a symbol of chaos
subdued by God (Job 41; Ps 74:14; Isa 27:1).

### Behemoth

Hebrew בְּהֵמוֹת — "the great beast" (plural of majesty of "animal") described in
Job 40; a colossal land creature.

### Rahab (monster)

Hebrew רַהַב — "arrogance, storm"; a chaos sea monster and poetic name for Egypt
(Ps 87:4; Isa 51:9). Not the woman Rahab of Jericho (different Hebrew spelling).

### Lilith

Hebrew לִילִית — a night creature haunting ruins (Isa 34:14); in later Jewish lore
a female demon. KJV: "screech owl".

### Azazel

Hebrew עֲזָאזֵל — the destination of the second goat on the Day of Atonement (Lev
16:8–26); probably a wilderness demon or "complete removal". KJV: "the
scapegoat".

### Cherub · cherubim

Hebrew כְּרוּב, plural כְּרוּבִים — mighty winged guardian beings: at Eden's gate, on
the ark's cover, carrying God's throne (Ezek 1; 10). Not chubby baby angels.

### Seraphim

Hebrew שְׂרָפִים — "burning ones": six-winged beings around God's throne (Isa 6).

## Worship, Torah and priesthood

### Torah

Hebrew תּוֹרָה — "instruction, teaching, direction" (from _yarah_, "to point, to
teach"); God's instruction given through Moses, and the five books of Moses. Far
wider than "law" in the legal sense. KJV: "law".

### Selah

Hebrew סֶלָה — a musical or liturgical note in Psalms and Habakkuk; meaning
uncertain — perhaps "lift up", a pause or an interlude.

### Asherah

Hebrew אֲשֵׁרָה — Canaanite mother-goddess, and the wooden pole or sacred tree that
represented her. KJV: "grove".

### Baal

Hebrew בַּעַל — "lord, owner, husband"; the Canaanite storm god, Israel's great
rival cult.

### Ashtoreth

Hebrew form of _Astarte_, Canaanite goddess of fertility and war; the vowels are
mockingly borrowed from _boshet_, "shame".

### Molech

Hebrew מֹלֶךְ — a god worshipped with child sacrifice (Lev 18:21; Jer 32:35); the
vowels likely mock _melekh_, "king".

### Chemosh · Milcom

National gods of Moab (Chemosh) and Ammon (Milcom).

### Nazirite

Hebrew נָזִיר — "one set apart": a person under a vow to God — no wine, no
haircut, no contact with the dead (Num 6). KJV: "Nazarite". Not the same as
"Nazarene" (from Nazareth).

### Ephod

Hebrew אֵפוֹד — a priestly apron/vest; the high priest's ephod carried the
breastpiece with the Urim and Thummim.

### Urim and Thummim

Hebrew "lights and perfections" — sacred lots kept in the high priest's
breastpiece, used to ask God's decision (Exod 28:30).

### Teraphim

Hebrew תְּרָפִים — household idols / family gods (Gen 31:19; 1 Sam 19:13).

### Shofar

Hebrew שׁוֹפָר — a ram's-horn trumpet, blown for alarms, war and holy days. KJV:
"trumpet", "cornet".

### Man-hu

Hebrew מָן הוּא — "What is it?" — Israel's question on first seeing the bread from
heaven (Exod 16:15); the origin of the name _man_, "manna".

### Yakhin and Bo'az

Names of the two bronze pillars at the front of Solomon's temple (1 Kgs 7:21):
"he establishes" and "in him is strength". KJV: "Jachin" and "Boaz".

## Psalm headings (musical terms)

### Maskil

Hebrew מַשְׂכִּיל — probably "a skilful / instructive song" (Ps 32 and others). KJV:
"Maschil".

### Miktam

Hebrew מִכְתָּם — uncertain; perhaps "inscription" or "golden (precious) poem" (Ps
16; 56–60). KJV: "Michtam".

### Shiggaion · Shigyonot

Hebrew — perhaps "a wandering, passionate song / lament" (Ps 7; Hab 3:1).

### Gittith

Hebrew — a tune or instrument, perhaps "from Gath" or "of the winepress" (Ps 8;
81; 84).

### Neginah · Neginot

Hebrew — "stringed instruments" (Ps 4; 61). KJV: "Neginoth".

### Alamoth · Sheminith

Musical settings: _Alamoth_ "young women" (high voices, soprano), _Sheminith_
"the eighth" (lower octave / eight-stringed).

### Al-tashcheth

Hebrew "Do not destroy" — the name of a tune (Ps 57–59; 75).

### Muth-labben · Jonath-elem-rehokim · Shoshannim · Shushan-eduth · Mahalath · Leannoth

Names of tunes or settings: "death of the son", "silent dove of far-off places",
"lilies", "lily of testimony", "sickness / flute", "for affliction / to be
sung".

### Higgaion

Hebrew — "meditation, murmuring, resounding music" (Ps 9:16).

### Jeduthun

One of David's chief musicians; his name heads several psalms (Ps 39; 62; 77).

### Qinot

Hebrew קִינוֹת — "laments, dirges" — the book of laments mentioned in 2 Chr 35:25.

## Calendar and feasts

### Aviv · Nisan

First month (March–April): _Aviv_ "fresh ears of grain" is the old name; _Nisan_
the Babylonian-era name. Month of Passover. KJV: "Abib".

### Ziv · Sivan

_Ziv_ "brightness" — 2nd month (April–May), old name. _Sivan_ — 3rd month
(May–June).

### Elul · Etanim · Bul · Kislev · Tebet · Shevat · Adar

Months: _Elul_ 6th (Aug–Sep); _Etanim_ "ever-flowing streams" 7th (Sep–Oct,
later Tishri); _Bul_ 8th (Oct–Nov); _Kislev_ 9th (Nov–Dec); _Tebet_ 10th
(Dec–Jan); _Shevat_ 11th (Jan–Feb); _Adar_ 12th (Feb–Mar).

### Sabbath

Hebrew שַׁבָּת — "ceasing, rest": the seventh day (Friday sunset to Saturday
sunset), set apart for rest.

### Passover

Hebrew _Pesach_ — "passing over / sparing": the feast remembering the exodus
from Egypt, 14 Nisan.

### Booths

Hebrew _Sukkot_ — the autumn harvest feast when Israel lives in huts (Lev
23:34). KJV: "tabernacles".

### Purim

Hebrew "lots" — feast celebrating the rescue of the Jews in Esther's time (Esth
9:26).

## Places and regions

### Negev

Hebrew נֶגֶב — "the dry land": the southern desert region of Judah. KJV: "the
south".

### Shephelah

Hebrew שְׁפֵלָה — "lowland": the low foothills between Judah's hill country and the
coastal plain. KJV: "the vale", "the low country".

### Arabah

Hebrew עֲרָבָה — "desert plain": the rift valley of the Jordan, the Dead Sea and
down to the Red Sea. KJV: "the plain".

### Wadi

Arabic/Hebrew _nachal_ — a stream bed that flows only in the rainy season. KJV:
"brook", "river".

### Zion

Hebrew צִיּוֹן — the fortified hill of Jerusalem captured by David; poetic name for
Jerusalem and God's dwelling.

### Mizpah

Hebrew "watchtower" — the name of several towns.

### Ebenezer

Hebrew "stone of help" (1 Sam 7:12).

### Bethel

Hebrew "house of _El_ (God)" — Jacob's dream place (Gen 28:19).

### Peniel · Penuel

Hebrew "face of _El_" — where Jacob wrestled with God (Gen 32:30).

### Armageddon

Hebrew _Har Megiddo_ — "mountain of Megiddo" (Rev 16:16), site of many ancient
battles.

### Paradise

Persian loanword "walled garden, park" (Luke 23:43).

## Names with a message

### Ichabod

Hebrew "Where is the glory?" / "no glory" (1 Sam 4:21).

### Ben-oni

Hebrew "son of my sorrow" — Rachel's name for her dying son; Jacob renamed him
Benjamin, "son of the right hand" (Gen 35:18).

### Shear-Yashuv

Hebrew "a remnant will return" — Isaiah's son (Isa 7:3).

### Maher-Shalal-Hash-Baz

Hebrew "swift is the spoil, speedy the plunder" — Isaiah's son (Isa 8:1–3).

### Lo-Ruchamah · Ruchamah

Hebrew "not pitied / shown no mercy" and "pitied" — Hosea's daughter (Hos 1:6;
2:1). KJV: "Lo-ruhamah", "Ruhamah".

### Lo-Ammi · Ammi

Hebrew "not my people" and "my people" — Hosea's son (Hos 1:9; 2:1).

### Hamon-Gog

Hebrew "the horde of Gog" — the valley where Gog's army is buried (Ezek 39:11).

### Gog and Magog

Gog, a ruler "of the land of Magog", leading the final nations against God's
people (Ezek 38–39; Rev 20:8).

### Tsaphenat-Paneach

Egyptian name given to Joseph by Pharaoh, probably "God speaks and he lives"
(Gen 41:45). KJV: "Zaphnath-paaneah".

### Avrekh

Egyptian/Semitic shout before Joseph's chariot, perhaps "Attention!" or "Bow the
knee!" (Gen 41:43). KJV: "Bow the knee".

### Perez

Hebrew "breach, breaking through" (Gen 38:29; 2 Sam 6:8).

### Nachamu

Hebrew "Comfort (plural)!" — the doubled opening of Isa 40:1, "Nachamu, nachamu,
my people".

### Yeshuah

Hebrew יְשׁוּעָה — "salvation, rescue" (Jonah 2:9); the same root as the name
_Yeshua_.

### Tehillah

Hebrew תְּהִלָּה — "praise" (Isa 60:18); plural _Tehillim_ is the Hebrew name of the
book of Psalms.

### MENE, TEKEL, PARSIN

Aramaic writing on the wall (Dan 5:25): "counted, weighed, divided" — also the
weights mina, shekel and half-mina.

## Measures and money

### Cubit

Hebrew _ammah_ — forearm length, elbow to fingertip: about 45 cm (18 in).

### Shekel

Hebrew שֶׁקֶל — "weight": about 11.4 g, of silver as money. A _gerah_ is 1/20
shekel.

### Mina · Talent

_Mina_ = 50–60 shekels (about 0.6 kg); _talent_ = 60 minas (about 34 kg) — a
huge sum.

### Ephah · Homer · Omer · Kor · Bath · Hin

Measures of volume: _ephah_ ≈ 22 l (dry); _homer_ and _kor_ = 10 ephahs ≈ 220 l
("a donkey-load"); _omer_ = 1/10 ephah ≈ 2.2 l; _bath_ ≈ 22 l (liquid); _hin_ ≈
3.7 l (liquid).

### Denarius

Roman silver coin — a labourer's daily wage (Matt 20:2). Plural _denarii_. KJV:
"penny".

### Lepton · Quadrans · Assarion

Tiny coins: _lepton_ the smallest Jewish copper coin ("the widow's mite", Mark
12:42); two _lepta_ = one _quadrans_; an _assarion_ ≈ 1/16 denarius.
